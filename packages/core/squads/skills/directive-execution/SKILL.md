# Directive execution

A directive is the boss's decision, handed to you by the Secretary. It quotes the
boss's own words. You acknowledge it, plan it, and run it. Worked examples are in
[reference.md](reference.md).

## 1. Acknowledge

Within the first run, comment on the directive in one or two lines: what you
understood the objective to be, what done means, and the date. If the Secretary's
reading differs from the boss's quote, say so in the comment. Do not start from the
Secretary's paraphrase.

## 2. Plan

- Read the boss's words and the constraints in the directive.
- Read the squad's project and the issues already in it that touch the objective.
- Write the plan as a short list of stages, each with its done condition and an owner.
- Name what you do not know and what would change the plan.

## 3. Decompose

- Create one issue per piece of work in the squad's project, assigned to the member
  who owns it. Keep each issue small enough to finish.
- Link each issue to the directive as its parent, so the Secretary can see the tree.
- Put the dependencies in the issues, not in a side note.

## 4. Run it through the workflow

- Let the project's workflow carry each issue through its steps. Follow the workflow
  brief for status moves and handoffs.
- Do not skip a review or a test the workflow requires because the directive is
  urgent. If the workflow would make the date impossible, say so in a comment and
  escalate.
- Do not change the scope the boss gave. A better idea goes to the Secretary as a
  question.

## 5. Report milestones

Post a short comment on the directive at each milestone: a stage done, a risk that
changed, a date that moved. Say what is done, what is next, and the date you now
expect. Do not write a report form; the Secretary reads your comments.

## 6. Escalate a blocker

Raise a blocker as soon as it is real, not at the deadline. Ask the Secretary in a
comment on the directive, and say:

- What is blocked, and by what.
- What you tried.
- The decision or fact you need, and from whom.
- What happens to the date if the answer is late.

The Secretary answers from what it knows, asks another leader, or brings it to the boss.
You do not ask the boss directly.

## 7. Close

- Close the directive when its done condition is met, not when the last issue is
  closed. Check the done condition against the evidence.
- Comment with the outcome: what was delivered, the links to the evidence, what was
  left out and why, and any follow-up that needs a decision.
- If you could not finish, say so and say what remains. Do not mark it done.

## Rules

- A directive does not approve a proposal, a stage gate, or a launch. Those keep their
  own decision flow. If one is needed, escalate it to the Secretary.
- Spend within the squad's budget only. A spend above it goes to a proposal.
- Keep the Secretary informed; do not wait for it to ask.
