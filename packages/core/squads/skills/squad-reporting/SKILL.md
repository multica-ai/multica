# Squad reporting

The Secretary asked you, the leader, for an answer about your squad. Answer it
yourself. You may ask members to look at things, but the assessment is yours. Your
answer is for a busy boss, read through the Secretary. Worked examples of good and weak
answers are in [reference.md](reference.md).

## Gather cheaply first

- Read the issues in the squad, their status, and their last comments.
- Read the recent runs and their failures; the latest pull requests and reviews.
- Read the documents the squad published.
- Ask a member only for what you cannot read: a judgment, a number that lives in
  someone's head, a check you cannot run yourself.
- Note what you did not check. You will report it.

## Dimensions

Choose the dimensions that answer the question. Not every report needs all of them.

- **Completion.** Against the plan or the acceptance list, as a range with the reason
  for each end. What remains, in items. Never a single percentage with nothing behind
  it.
- **Schedule.** Ahead, on track, or slipping, and the leading signs: stale work, a
  review queue, repeated retries, blocked time.
- **Quality.** Failed runs, rework, review findings, test results, defects that got out
  when you know of them.
- **Risk.** Each risk with its cause, likelihood, impact, owner, a proposed response,
  and whether it needs the boss. Mark what you observed apart from what you suspect.
- **Dependencies.** On other squads, people, or providers, and requests that are
  overdue.
- **Capacity and cost.** Who is stretched, and the budget spent and projected, when it
  matters for the question.
- **Outcomes.** For squads with goals: the metric, its trend, and its reading.
- **Confidence.** How sure you are, what you have not checked, and what would raise it.
- **Recommendations.** Concrete, with an owner, and what you will do without the boss.

## Calibrate confidence

- A range is honest. A single number is a claim you must back.
- Name the evidence the range rests on. A conclusion without its source is a guess.
- High confidence needs a check you ran. Otherwise say medium or low, and why.
- "I did not check" is a complete sentence. Use it when it is true.

## Evidence habit

Cite the issue, run, pull request, document, or comment each claim rests on, as a plain
link. A claim you cannot link is marked as an inference.

## Floor chiefs

A floor chief rolls up its leaders' answers. It does not replace them.

- Ask each squad leader on the floor for its part, unless its own issues and runs
  already answer the question.
- Read each answer critically. Challenge a weak one, as the Secretary would.
- Report progress by squad. A quiet squad is one line.
- Give one floor-level view: the overall schedule and risk for the floor, the risks
  ranked with owners, the dependencies between squads on the floor, and the
  disagreements between them.
- Name what you could not check.

## Where it goes

Post the answer as a comment on your child issue and close the issue. If the answer is
long, write a document and link it from the comment. Ask the Secretary, not the boss,
for anything you need from the boss.

## Rules

- Report on the squad, not on individuals. Name a person only where the record shows
  that person's own action.
- Do not change the squad's charter, instructions, or budget.
- Do not invent a number to fill a gap.
