# Business metrics

The Secretary asked you how your squad's business outcomes are moving. Outcomes are
not the same as activity: a busy squad can miss its goal, and a quiet one can be
on track. Read the metric, not the effort. Your answer goes to a busy boss through the
Secretary. Worked examples are in [reference.md](reference.md).

## Find the goals

- Read your squad's active key results. The briefing lists them as **Your goals this
  period**. Use those names and targets exactly; do not restate them from memory.
- Check the goal's status and its period. A goal in `draft` has no outcome yet. A
  goal past its period is `achieved` or `missed`, and you say which.
- A key result without a target or a start value cannot show progress. Say so, and
  do not invent one.

## Read the trend, not the last point

- Take the latest reading and the readings before it, for the same scope. A single
  point is a value, not a trend.
- Compare the progress with the straight line from the period's start to its end.
  Ahead of the line, on the line, or behind it: that is the reading.
- Say which way the metric is moving over the last two or three readings, and how
  far. "Up 4 since Monday" is a trend; "good" is not.
- Built-in metrics come from the platform's nightly calculation. Manual readings and
  webhook readings come from a person or a system. Say which kind you read, because a
  manual reading may be stale.

## Say what the reading means

- Separate the metric from the goal. A metric can move while the goal does not, if
  the metric is only a proxy. Say when you think that is the case.
- Name the cause you can see in the record: a launch, a change in price, a stalled
  initiative. Mark a cause you suspect as a suspicion.
- A goal weighted heavily that is off track matters more than several small ones on
  track. Lead with the weighted picture when the Secretary asked for the whole squad.
- Do not call a goal "at risk" or "off track" when the platform's confidence says
  otherwise without a reason. If you disagree with the platform, say why in one line.

## State your confidence

- A reading you took from the platform is firm. A reading a member typed in, or one
  that has not updated for a period, is less firm. Say which.
- Give the confidence as high, medium or low, and the reason. High needs a reading
  from this period that you checked.
- "I did not check" is a complete sentence. Use it when it is true, for example for
  a metric you could not open.

## Name what is not measured

Every outcome answer ends with the gaps.

- A goal with no metric behind it, or a metric with no readings for the period.
- An outcome that has no leading signal yet, so the boss will not see it move until
  late.
- A cost or revenue figure the provider does not report, which the platform cannot
  count.
- Anything you would need a person outside the company to confirm.

## Rules

- Never invent a reading, a target or a trend. If the data is missing, say so.
- Do not report a squad's activity as an outcome. Issues closed is effort.
- Do not change a goal, a target, a metric or its confidence override. Those belong
  to the boss and the admins.
- Cite the metric key, the scope and the reading time in plain text, so the Secretary
  can open the same record.
