# Business metrics: good and weak examples

Each example answers the same question from the Secretary: "How are the Q4 revenue goal
and its key results moving for the growth squad?" The goal has two key results: monthly
recurring revenue (target 120,000, start 80,000, weight 6) and trial-to-paid conversion
(target 6 percent, start 3 percent, weight 4). The period started on 1 October and ends
on 31 December.

## Trend

Weak:

> Revenue is doing well.

Good:

> MRR (`mrr`, company scope, manual readings) is 101,000 at the 4 November reading, against
> a straight-line expectation of about 95,000 for this point in the period. Progress is
> 52 percent. The last three readings were 96,000, 98,500 and 101,000, so it is rising about
> 2,500 a week. At that pace it reaches roughly 120,000, at the target, by the end of December.

## Reading

Weak:

> The goal is on track.

Good:

> The goal is on track in aggregate: weighted progress is 37 percent against a line of about
> 38 percent at this point in the period. The conversion key result is the one to watch. It
> is at 13 percent progress, about 25 points behind its own line, at 3.4 percent against a
> target of 6. MRR, at 52 percent and ahead, carries six of the ten weights, so the goal's
> position rests on it. If MRR slows, the goal goes at risk quickly.

## Confidence

Weak:

> High confidence.

Good:

> Medium confidence. The MRR reading is from this week and I checked it against the billing
> export linked in the last comment. The conversion reading is a manual figure from 14
> October and has not been updated since, so I cannot say it is current.

## What is not measured

Weak:

> Nothing else to report.

Good:

> Not measured: churn, which has no metric in this goal, so a fall in MRR from cancellations
> would look like slow growth. Expansion revenue is not reported by the provider and is
> left out of the MRR figure. I did not check the trial sign-ups behind the conversion rate;
> they are in a source the platform cannot read.

## A metric that is a proxy

Weak:

> Signups are up 30 percent, so the goal is fine.

Good:

> Signups are up 30 percent (`signups`, squad scope), but the goal measures paid revenue, and
> signups do not move it on their own. The conversion key result is the one that matters,
> and it has not moved. I suggest the boss reads the conversion figure, not the signups.

## A goal with no reading

Weak:

> Still working on it.

Good:

> No reading yet for the churn key result, so its progress is unknown and it does not count
> in the goal's weighted progress. The metric exists but has no readings for this period. The
> owner of the reading is not named in the record. I would need the boss or the finance squad
> to supply the first figure.
