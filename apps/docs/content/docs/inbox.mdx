The inbox collects the work activity that concerns you and flags where something changed that needs your attention. It isn't a full activity log; the full story stays on the corresponding issue.

## What lands in the inbox

Common notifications include:

- an issue is assigned to you, or moved away from you;
- an issue you subscribe to gets a new comment, or its assignee, status, priority, or dates change;
- someone @-mentions you in an issue description or comment;
- an issue or comment you created gets a reaction;
- an agent run fails on an issue you subscribe to;
- [creating an issue through an agent](/issues#create-with-agent) completes or fails;
- an autopilot you subscribe to creates a new issue, or is paused after consecutive failures.

Your own actions don't notify you. Multiple notifications on the same issue merge into one entry; open it to see the issue's recent changes.

## Sources of automatic subscription

These participants subscribe to an issue automatically:

- the issue's creator;
- a new assignee;
- anyone who comments;
- anyone newly @-mentioned in the issue description;
- subscribers preconfigured in an autopilot.

Changing the assignee does not remove existing subscriptions. Subscribe or unsubscribe anytime on the issue page; after unsubscribing, ordinary updates stop reaching your inbox, while direct @-mentions still notify you individually.

<Callout type="info">
When a sub-issue's status changes, subscribers of the parent issue are notified as well. Sub-issue comments, priority, and date changes do not bubble up to the parent.
</Callout>

## Agents don't use the inbox

The inbox is only for workspace members. Even when an agent is the assignee or a subscriber, it never reads inbox notifications.

@-mentioning an agent in a comment, assigning an issue to it, or triggering an autopilot creates a run directly; no notification is left for the agent. `@all` notifies members only.

## Sections

The inbox lists each issue once, under whose turn it is:

- **Needs you** — the issue is waiting on you. You were @-mentioned or assigned, an agent run you started finished, failed, or was cancelled, or something happened on an issue you created or are assigned to: a new comment, a failed run, or a status that waits on a person, such as `in_review` or `blocked`. Each row says why, for example **Ready for review**, **Waiting for your answer**, or **Run failed**.
- **Working** — an agent is working on the issue because you asked: you assigned it, commented, or reran it. The row shows the agent and how long the run has been going, or **Queued**. Runs you didn't start yourself, such as an autopilot's or another member's, aren't listed.
- **Updates** — everything else: activity on issues you subscribe to that doesn't wait on you. Only unread ones are listed; an update moves to **Archived** once you've read it.
- **Snoozed** — issues you put away until a later time.

An issue that fits more than one section appears in the first of them, in the order above. **Updates** and **Snoozed** stay folded until you open them; each header shows how many issues it holds.

The inbox badge counts the issues under **Needs you**. Reading one doesn't take it off the count: it stays until you hand it to an agent, snooze it, or archive it.

When your comment or assignment starts an agent run, the issue leaves **Needs you** and shows under **Working**. When the run finishes, fails, or is cancelled, it comes back to **Needs you**, unless the issue is already closed.

A cancelled run comes back as **Run cancelled**. If you stopped it yourself, the row is already read and says **You stopped this run**; if someone else did, it is unread and names them. It doesn't come back while another run you started is active on the issue, as when you rerun it: the issue stays under **Working**.

## Handle notifications

Selecting a notification marks it as read. Under **Needs you**, reading isn't handling: the row stays until you act on the issue. Archive it when you're done, with **Archive** in the header of the open issue or `E`; archiving doesn't modify or delete the corresponding issue.

Once an issue reaches a status that hands it back to a person, its **Run failed** notifications archive automatically. Those statuses are `in_review`, custom statuses in the `started` category, and any status in the `done` or `closed` category. `blocked` is the exception: it hands the issue over too, but the work is still stuck, so the earlier failures are still worth reading.

An issue moved to a status in the `done` or `closed` category leaves everyone's inbox; what remains is one entry under **Updates** saying it closed.

Right-click a notification for **Mark as read** / **Mark as unread**, snoozing, and **Archive** (**Unarchive** in the Archived view). Mark as unread is the "I looked, but I'm not done with this" action: the notification goes back to unread, and it stays unread even while you keep it open; opening it again later marks it read as usual. Archived notifications keep their read state, so restoring one brings it back exactly as you left it. An update is the exception: it comes back unread, because a read update would leave again. **Delete** removes a notification for good, and is offered in the Archived view only.

Under **Updates**, reading is handling: open one and it moves to **Archived** when you move on to something else. **Clear** on the section header archives every update listed, read or not. Nothing leaves **Needs you** in bulk: an issue goes when you handle it, archive it, or snooze it.

Open **Archived** from the menu on the inbox title to browse older notifications and restore one. More records load as you scroll.

### Filter

The inbox filters by the issue: status, priority, project, assignee, label, and due date. A filter applies to every section, **Working** included. **Archived** can also be filtered by the notification itself: read state, type, sender, and when it was received. Its filters apply to the entire archive, including records that have not loaded yet.

### Snooze

Snooze an issue to put it away until a time you choose. Open it and use **Snooze** in the header (or press `H`), or right-click it in the list. The choices are **In 1 hour**, **Later today**, **Tomorrow**, and **Next Monday**; the header menu also has **Pick a time...** for any time within the next year.

A snoozed issue moves to **Snoozed** and stops counting on the badge. When the time comes, it returns to the section it was in, as unread. It comes back early if something new needs you on that issue, such as a mention or a failed run; activity that only lands under **Updates** doesn't bring it back. To bring it back yourself, use **Unsnooze** in the same menus.

## Tune notification types

Open **Settings → Notifications**. The page follows the inbox's sections:

- **Needs you** — mentions, assignments, runs you started, and activity on issues you created or are assigned to. These are always delivered. The one switch is **@all and @squad**: turn it off and group mentions are filed under **Updates** instead of **Needs you**.
- **Updates** — comments, status changes, assignment changes, priority and due date, new issues, and agent runs on issues you follow. Each can be turned off.

Turning a kind off doesn't stop the changes from being recorded on the issue — it only stops the inbox notifications. A few types, such as reactions, quick-create results, and autopilot pauses, have no switch and are always delivered.

System and browser notifications control desktop banners and don't change the inbox itself. From the inbox, only an unread notification under **Needs you** raises a banner; updates never do.
