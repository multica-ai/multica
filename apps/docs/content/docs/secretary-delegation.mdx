You work with one agent, the **Secretary**, in chat. Ask it a question that needs the squads, such as "Is the spring launch on track, and what would slip it?", and it does the rounds: it asks the leaders who own each part, reads their answers, challenges what looks thin, and comes back with one assessment and the decisions it needs from you. You decide in the same chat. The Secretary then hands each decision to the leader who will carry it out.

The daily brief is separate and unchanged. It is described in [Secretary and briefs](/secretary).

<Callout type="info">
This is how the on-demand flow is designed to work. Parts of it are still being built and verified. The runbook records what has been checked on a real machine, step by step.
</Callout>

## What you do

- **Ask.** Ask in the Secretary's chat as you would ask a chief of staff. Say what you want back when it matters: a number, a risk list, a decision list, a comparison, or an early warning.
- **Answer a clarifying question.** If the project or the period is unclear, the Secretary asks you one short question and waits. It does not guess and fan out.
- **Decide.** When the assessment lists decisions, reply in ordinary words, for example "D1: go with B, and hold D2". The Secretary repeats back what it understood for each decision and asks whether it is wrong. Confirm with a single "yes".
- **Step in when a leader asks for you.** A leader that needs your decision asks the Secretary first. The Secretary answers it, asks another leader, or brings the question to you in chat with its recommendation.

You do not fill in forms, and you do not have to read every squad's status to get an answer.

## What the Secretary delegates

The Secretary does not do a department's work. It decides whom to ask, writes each ask for that person in its own words, and reads what comes back.

| The Secretary decides | Example |
|---|---|
| Whom to ask. A narrow question goes to one squad leader. A floor-wide question goes to the floor chief when the floor has one, and to its leaders when it does not. | "How is research doing?" goes to the Research floor chief. |
| Whom not to ask. A quiet squad whose answer it can already read is left out, and it says so. | "Growth Lifecycle has no open work, so I did not ask it." |
| What to ask. It writes the question, why it matters, the period, what it already knows, and when it needs the answer. | A leader is asked for a completion range and the risks it owns, not for a status summary. |

Each addressee gets a child issue under a request issue in the Secretary desk project. You can open any of them. Leaders answer in a comment on their issue, and close it when they have answered.

The leaders run their own analysis. They read their issues, runs and pull requests, may ask a member to look at something, and write their own assessment. The Secretary does not pre-digest their facts for them.

## How the Secretary reads the answers

When every child is closed, or the request's deadline passes, the Secretary reads each answer critically and then compares them. It:

- opens the issue, comment or run that a claim rests on, when a claim is important or too tidy;
- challenges a weak answer in a comment on the leader's issue, and the leader answers again;
- looks across the answers: where the risk is concentrated, which squad others depend on, and whether the progress claims add up to the date;
- compares with its earlier briefs and assessments.

A challenge is a comment, so the leader is woken to answer it, and you can read the exchange. A limit on challenges stops a runaway: after a set number on one issue the Secretary closes it as unresolved and says so. The limit is eight challenges per child issue; a ninth is refused.

The assessment comes back in the same chat. It leads with the answer, names the decisions, ranks the risks with an owner for each, says what leaders disagreed about, and says what it did not check. A quiet question gets a short answer. It also reports what the request cost, in runs and tokens.

Where a leader did not answer in time, the Secretary says so. It does not fill the gap with a guess.

## Directives

When you decide something in chat, the Secretary can hand it to a leader as a **directive**. A directive is an issue for the leader or chief, in the project the squad holds. It carries:

- your own words, quoted from the chat message that asked for it;
- the objective, the constraints you gave, and the priority and date;
- the report it came from, and what the Secretary thinks "done" means.

The leader acknowledges the directive in a comment, says in a line what it understood, breaks the objective into issues in the project, and runs them through the project's workflow. It keeps the Secretary informed at milestones, and raises a blocker as soon as it is real.

Three rules keep this safe:

- **Only you create work this way.** A directive is accepted only from the Secretary, and only in a run started by a chat message from an owner or admin of the workspace. A scheduled routine, an autopilot or a wakeup cannot create one.
- **Your words are kept.** The chat message and its quote are stored on the directive, and the leader sees them.
- **Some decisions keep their own flow.** A directive cannot approve a proposal or a stage gate, and it cannot start a launch. Those keep the **Approve** and **Reject** buttons. The Secretary says so when you ask for one of them in chat.

<Callout type="info">
A proposal from a squad, asking you for money, scope or an exception, still uses the proposal flow. See [Proposals](/proposals).
</Callout>

## Questions from leaders

A leader that needs something it cannot get from its own squad comments on the directive or item issue and mentions the Secretary. The Secretary is then run as an ordinary agent. It either answers from what it knows (your earlier words, the knowledge base, another leader's answer), asks another leader, or brings the question to you.

You are asked only what the Secretary cannot settle. If you want to talk to a leader directly, open the leader's chat or issue yourself. Nothing prevents it.

## Where the skills are

The Secretary and the leaders work from skills. They are workspace skills, edited on the [Skills](/skills) page like any other, and they take effect on the next run.

| Skill | Used by | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `secretary-analysis` | Secretary | How to understand a question, choose whom to ask, write an ask, read critically and challenge, analyse across squads, write the assessment, number decisions, and turn them into directives. |
| `squad-reporting` | Squad leaders and floor chiefs | The dimensions a leader can cover (completion, schedule, quality, risk, dependencies, capacity, outcomes, confidence, recommendations), with examples of good and weak answers. |
| `directive-execution` | Squad leaders and floor chiefs | Acknowledging a directive, planning it, breaking it into issues, using the project workflow, milestones, escalation and closing. |
| `business-metrics` | Squads with goals | How to read goals and key results, the trend against the period, and how to say what is not measured. |

You tune the behaviour in the skill, in plain words: "always lead with decisions", "do not report on the Growth squad unless it is red", "use our risk scale". The next run uses the new text. The starter version is kept, so you can restore it.

<Callout type="warning">
The starter skills are meant to be installed once. A later run of company setup should leave your edits in place.
</Callout>

## The org map

The Secretary knows the structure of the company from the org map, not from memory. It lists the floors and their chiefs, the squads and their leaders, members, the squad's project and workflow, and the open work in each squad. Any member can read it:

```bash
multica company map
multica company map --floor <floor-id>
```

Add `--output json` for machine-readable output. The endpoint is `GET /api/company/org-map`.

## Limits

- The Secretary reads the whole workspace, and it can ask any squad. It cannot change an issue's status or assignee, delete anything, or decide a proposal.
- Company controls apply to the leaders' runs. When the company is paused, or in quiet hours, the Secretary's answer says it is partial and why.
- Budgets and holds apply to every run as usual.
- The number of squads it asks in one request (20), of open requests (8), and of directives per chat message (5) is capped. These limits are fixed for now. Work under a request may nest at most two levels below it (the Secretary's addressee, then the leader under it); a deeper issue is refused. Challenges are capped at eight per child issue.
